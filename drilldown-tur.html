How TUR is measured

Closed

Grayed-out truck — Repairs, Sold, or Personnel (no crew). Doesn't count against you.

Open

Staffed truck with no job. The actionable gap — a crew and a truck with nothing to do.

Booked %

Booked ÷ Available (available = total − closed). Are the trucks working?

Fullness %

Booked hours ÷ (available trucks × 8-hr day). Are they full, or a pile of half-day jobs?

The two days already tell the story. Tuesday was slammed — every available truck booked and ~84% full. Wednesday booked 85% but only filled 53% of the day: two staffed trucks sat open and most jobs were 3–4 hr half-days. Booked% tells you if trucks are working; Fullness% tells you how much capacity is leaking out in half-days. Both matter, and they move independently.

Decision: how do we source TUR going forward?

TUR is the most valuable metric here (it drives NOI) and the hardest to pull. Two honest realities from building this prototype:

  • Scale. It's a per-day, per-branch read. A full week × 5 branches = ~35 separate Move Manager pulls every Monday. That's not sustainable to do by hand.
  • Accuracy. Closed / unbooked / booked counts read cleanly. But partial-vs-full needs each truck's total hours, and busy days stack two jobs on one truck (Sat Aug 1: 21 jobs across 15 trucks) — the flat job list can't attribute those to the right truck. Partial/full specifically needs the underlying schedule data.

Recommendation: ask Matisen for a simple weekly Move Manager export — per branch per day: total trucks, closed, and each truck's booked hours. That one feed turns ~35 fragile manual pulls into one clean import and makes TUR (plus the per-mover OT view) genuinely weekly-sustainable. Until then, I can grind it manually branch-by-branch if you want it now — Dallas is half-done above.
